## WHAT TO STEAL AND ADAPT FOR K.I.N.D.

K.I.N.D. is a B2B AI sales platform targeting African and UK SMEs, with three agents: FIGSY (sales), Milla (brain/intelligence), and Vida (connector). Here is what to directly take, adapt, or avoid from ClickUp's playbook.

---

### TIKTOK / REELS — STEAL IMMEDIATELY

**Steal: The "persona-as-protagonist" comedy series**
ClickUp doesn't say "here's our HR feature." They make HR *the character* in the sketch. For K.I.N.D., the equivalent:
- Series: **"A day in the life of a sales rep who uses ancient tools"** — show the pain of cold outreach without AI. Deadpan, observational. FIGSY "solves" it in the last 5 seconds.
- Series: **"The Boss's Questions"** — an SME owner asking increasingly impossible questions of their sales team. Milla answers them all instantly. Comedy + product proof in 45 seconds.
- Series: **"Vida, sort this out"** — Vida the connector fixes a chaotic networking/follow-up situation that a human is visibly drowning in. The relief is comedic.

**Steal: Hire actors, not experts**
Don't put your founders or product team on camera as the leads. Cast comedic performers or natural storytellers who can embody the SME owner archetype. Product team can be in the background or referenced, not front and centre.

**Steal: The numbered series format**
"Sales reality check #1", "Sales reality check #7" — the number signals ongoing content, creates anticipation, builds watch time from people who want to catch up on previous episodes. ClickUp's HR Training Module series benefits enormously from this.

**Steal: Persona-specific hashtag layering**
For K.I.N.D.: #SMEsales + #saleslife + #businessafrica + #ukbusiness + #foundersofinstagram + #salestips — mix 2 broad trending tags with 2–3 persona-specific tags per post.

**Adapt: The "escape from work" principle**
ClickUp's TikTok works because it gives workers a break from thinking about work. For K.I.N.D.'s audience (African and UK SME founders/sales teams), the equivalent emotional release is: *"someone finally gets how chaotic this is."* The comedy should be rooted in recognisable SME pain — chasing invoices, losing track of leads, copy-pasting the same pitch 40 times — not generic sales content.

---

### LINKEDIN — STEAL IMMEDIATELY

**Steal: Founder personal brand as the primary LinkedIn channel**
K.I.N.D.'s company page will underperform any founder's personal page at this stage. Post from founder profiles first. Product updates, customer wins, founder philosophy, failures and recovery — all of it. The K.I.N.D. company page should amplify and reshare.

**Steal: Meta-content about the product category**
Zeb Evans and Cunningham post about *how* you should think about productivity — not just "use ClickUp." For K.I.N.D.: post about *how* AI sales agents change the economics of SME sales. Posts like:
- "A 3-person sales team using AI closes like a 12-person team. Here's the maths."
- "Why Nigerian SMEs are skipping CRM and going straight to AI agents."
- "What FIGSY does in 8 minutes that your SDR does in 3 days."

These are thought leadership posts that educate the category and position K.I.N.D. as the authoritative voice — without being a product ad.

**Steal: Employee + customer story posts**
Short narrative posts (250–400 words) about a specific customer's win. Format: Problem → What they tried before → How they use K.I.N.D. → Specific result (pipeline built, deals closed, hours saved).

**Adapt: LinkedIn posting frequency**
ClickUp concentrates on TikTok + LinkedIn. For K.I.N.D., given the B2B African + UK SME target: **LinkedIn is the primary channel.** Post 3–4 times per week from founder profiles. Company page: 2–3 times per week.

### INSTAGRAM — ADAPT

**Steal: The comedy spinoff account model**
ClickUp's @clickupcomedy has *more* followers than @clickup. Consider building **@KINDsaleslife** or **@AIvsManualSales** as a comedy/entertainment account separate from the main brand account. This lets you experiment with tone without diluting the main brand, and if a sketch goes viral it doesn't need to "match" the main brand aesthetic.

**Steal: Team culture posts that show the humans behind the AI**
The paradox of an AI platform is that customers want to know the humans. Post team behind-the-scenes content that shows the people building FIGSY, Milla, and Vida. The AI agents have personality — let the team post from the *agents'* POV too (FIGSY has opinions, Milla explains things, Vida makes introductions).

**Adapt: Product screenshots for a different audience**
ClickUp posts lots of product UI and it works. For K.I.N.D., product demo content on Instagram should be short-form video (30 seconds) showing FIGSY finding a lead, Milla qualifying it, Vida sending the outreach — the whole flow in a Reel. Not a static screenshot.

### COMMUNITY — BUILD NOW, NOT LATER

**Steal: The tiered community model**
Build a **K.I.N.D. Verified** programme from day one:
- **Power User tier**: top users by platform activity get a badge and early access to new agent features
- **Ambassador tier**: founders and sales leaders who share K.I.N.D. on LinkedIn/TikTok get exclusive content, direct access to the product team, and co-marketing opportunities
- **Partner/Consultant tier**: African and UK-based sales consultants who implement K.I.N.D. for clients and earn referral commission

This is ClickUp's Verified model directly transposed. The African SME market especially benefits from a peer-to-peer trust layer — people buy based on who else in their network uses it.

**Steal: The affiliate programme structure**
Commission for *free signups* (not just paid) — this dramatically lowers the barrier for advocates to send referrals. For K.I.N.D.: $X for every workspace that activates (even free trial), larger commission for paid conversions. Use a platform like PartnerStack or FirstPromoter.

ClickUp has no Discord or Slack community — a gap. For K.I.N.D.'s African SME audience, **WhatsApp groups are the dominant community infrastructure**. A K.I.N.D.
