This is a genuinely strong idea and worth thinking through properly before building. Here's my take:

---

## What you're describing is a three-layer ICP system

**Layer 1 — Website intelligence (auto-fill baseline)**

On campaign creation, scrape the client's website — `/about`, `/services`, `/clients`. Claude reads it and generates a draft ICP: likely industries, decision-maker titles, geography, value signals. Present it as pre-filled cards the user confirms or adjusts. If the site is blocked or JS-heavy, graceful fallback to blank.

**Layer 2 — Conversational ICP builder**

Not a form. A chat. Milla asks:
- *"Who's your ideal client? Describe them like you'd describe them to a new salesperson."*
- *"What size company — 5 people or 200?"*
- *"What's the trigger event that makes them ready to buy right now?"*
- *"Any industries or geographies you want to avoid?"*
- *"Decision-maker or influencer — who signs off?"*

The output is structured ICP JSON that maps directly to Apollo search parameters. That's the hard part — natural language has to resolve cleanly to `job_titles[]`, `company_sizes[]`, `geographies[]` etc.

**Layer 3 — Campaign intent prompt (the "hunt" idea)**

This is separate from the baseline ICP and it's the most interesting part. The baseline ICP is who you *always* target. The hunt prompt is campaign-level intent:

> *"This week: CFOs at logistics companies in Nigeria, pain point = manual month-end reporting, don't touch anyone I've already emailed."*

FIGSY uses this to shift the search parameters *and* personalise the email angle differently. Same ICP, different attack.

---

## Why this is the right call

The blank ICP form is where most users stall. They don't know what "seniority_level: c_suite" means. They write "CEO" and wonder why the leads are wrong. A conversation forces them to articulate the real target — and a good set of questions surfaces things they wouldn't have typed into a form (trigger events, geography nuance, pain point framing).

The website scrape handles the cold start. The conversation handles the strategy. The hunt prompt handles the week-to-week.

---

## The one thing to solve before building

**Apollo field mapping.** Everything the AI extracts has to resolve to parameters Apollo actually accepts. If someone says "mid-market SaaS companies raising Series A," that maps to `company_sizes: ['51,200']`, `industries: ['SaaS']`, `keywords: ['Series A']` — but not perfectly. You need a mapping layer (probably another Claude call) that translates the conversation output into a validated search body before passing it to Apollo.

That's the tricky bit. Everything else is relatively straightforward.

---

## My recommendation when you're ready to build

Do it in this order:
1. Campaign intent prompt first — lowest risk, highest immediate impact, no scraping needed
2. Conversational ICP builder — replaces the form
3. Website scrape — nice to have, last because it's the most fragile
