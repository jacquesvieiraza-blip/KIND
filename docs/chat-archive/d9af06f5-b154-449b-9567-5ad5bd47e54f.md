## Best version of the product flow

Here's how I'd describe the ideal end-to-end experience:

**1. Onboarding (Day 1 — 10 minutes)**
New user signs up → guided directly to ICP Builder → answers 5 questions about who they sell to → KIND generates an ICP profile → immediately shows them 10 scored leads. First "aha" moment happens before they even leave the setup screen.

**2. Lead sourcing (one decision, not a CSV)**
From dashboard: "Find leads" → they describe their target → KIND searches Apollo, scores against ICP, and shows a ranked list. Company/ZoomInfo CSVs are an alternative path, not the primary one. User clicks "Approve all" or cherry-picks. No CSV export/import cycle.

**3. Consent (automated, not manual)**
Approved leads get a POPIA consent email automatically, not when someone clicks a button. Consent → automatic status update via webhook. No manual "mark as consented" except as a fallback. Client sees it happen in the activity feed in real time.

**4. FIGSY campaign (one button, not a wizard)**
Once there are consented leads, the dashboard nudges: "You have 12 consented leads — start a FIGSY campaign." Client names it, clicks Generate. KIND writes the 3-step sequence using their ICP and company context. They review and launch. Emails go out automatically, steps 2 and 3 follow as replies in the same thread.

**5. Replies → inbox (zero friction)**
Replies arrive in the portal inbox, classified (hot/warm/cold) by AI. Client sees them in one clean list — no switching between Gmail and KIND. One-click to mark as meeting booked, which feeds the KPI dashboard.

**6. Dashboard (a single scoreboard)**
Leads sourced → consented → emailed → replied → meetings booked. Live numbers, no refresh needed. Activity feed shows the last 20 events across all campaigns. Client knows at a glance if the machine is running.

**The gap between current and ideal:** steps 3 (auto-consent) and 6 (live numbers) are the two biggest friction points right now. Everything else is close.
