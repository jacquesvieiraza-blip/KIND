The journey — in the order it happens

01You

Tell us who your buyers are

We start with your targeting — the kind of company you sell to, the job titles you need, the places they are. You describe it in plain language; you don't fill in a database form.

What you do

Talk us through your best customers. Who closes fastest? Who pays the most? Who did you wish you'd found sooner?

What we do

Turn that into your targeting profile and show it back to you in writing before anything is bought.

Trainer note

Spend the most time here — everything downstream inherits it. Ask for three named customers they love and three deals they regret. Write the profile live on screen so they see it is theirs, not a template. Do not move on until they say "yes, that's who I want."

02Us

We find your people

We go and source roughly 200 people who match. That's deliberately double what your pack includes — you should be choosing from a real shortlist, not accepting whatever turns up.

Everyone is checked before they reach you: verified contact details, screened against opt-outs, and de-duplicated so we never hand you someone already in your CRM.

Trainer note

The 200-for-100 ratio is worth saying out loud — it's the difference between a shortlist and a dump. If they ask how long: first names usually land within a day or two, the rest follows.

03You

Your desk — approve or pass

Leads arrive on your dashboard masked: you see the company, the role, the fit and our reasoning — but not the person's contact details. That's on purpose. Looking is free, so look at all of them.

👍 Approve

You want this one. Their contact details unlock and our team starts working them straight away.

✕ Pass

Not for you. Costs nothing, and it teaches us what you don't want.

Approving is the only money event

It draws from your included 100 first. After those, it's a flat $4 — one price that covers unmasking the contact and the whole outreach we run on them. And if an email turns out to be dead, you are never charged for it.

Trainer note

Do this live. Have them approve one and pass one while you watch, so the first tap happens with you in the room rather than alone at 11pm. Watch for the client who is scared to approve — that is the single biggest failure mode, and it comes from thinking browsing costs money. Say it twice.

04Us

We write the outreach

For everyone you approve, we write the emails — the first message and the follow-ups — in your voice, referencing why that specific person was a fit.

You see the sequence before anything sends. Nothing goes out over your name that you haven't read.

Trainer note

Show them a real sequence on screen. Ask what sounds unlike them and change one line while they watch — it converts "AI wrote it" into "we wrote it together" faster than any reassurance.

05Us

Your sending inbox

Cold email only works from a mailbox with a good reputation, and reputation takes weeks to build. So we do two things at once:

  • From day one — you send from a warmed inbox we provide, so you're not waiting three weeks to start.
  • In the background — we buy and warm a sending domain and mailbox that look like your company. Around week three or four, your outreach moves onto it.
Why we never send from your main company address

Cold outreach attracts spam complaints — that's normal and unavoidable. If those land on the address you invoice from, your ordinary business email starts going to junk. Keeping the two separate protects the mailbox you can't afford to lose.

Trainer note

This is the part clients don't know they need, so it's where a lot of the $299 is justified. If they ask about ownership of the domain after they leave, say you'll confirm it in writing rather than answering on the spot — that policy is not settled.

06Us

Replies come back to one place

When someone answers, it lands in your inbox inside the portal — the whole thread, next to the lead it belongs to. You're never digging through a personal mailbox to find out who said what.

We handle the ordinary back-and-forth: follow-ups, chasing the ones who went quiet, and getting to a time in the diary.

Trainer note

Agree the handover point explicitly: at what moment do you stop and they take over? Most clients want the call booked before they touch it. Get the answer written down today.

08You

After your first 100

Once your included 100 are used, you top up your wallet whenever you choose and each approved lead draws a flat $4. No subscription, no retainer, no monthly minimum.

  • Your balance never expires.
  • We never charge automatically — approving is always your action.
  • A dead email is never charged, ever.
Trainer note

Don't oversell the top-up. The honest line is that the pack is the door and the repeat is the relationship — if the first hundred don't produce conversations, that's our problem to fix, not their budget to stretch.

What we need from you

Approve in batches, not one at a time

Outreach works on volume and rhythm. Approving twenty in one sitting beats approving two a day — it lets us build a proper sequence and gives the numbers a chance to mean something.

Keep going for a few weeks

A brand-new sending inbox starts gently and builds up. First replies usually arrive in the second or third week, not the first. The clients who do well are the ones who stayed in the habit past week one.

Tell us when a lead is wrong

Passing is useful; telling us why is far more useful. One sentence — "too small", "wrong country", "we already work with them" — and we tighten the targeting.

Two things to know up front, so they're never a surprise

Accounts that sit completely unused go dormant after about a month — nothing is lost and it's reversible, but we'd rather tell you now than have it surprise you. And there's a minimum number of approvals needed for outreach to be worth running at all; if you're approving fewer than about twenty a month, we should talk about whether the targeting is too narrow.

Trainer note — how to close the session

Finish with three things in writing: (1) how many leads they intend to approve in the first fortnight, (2) the handover point on replies, (3) a date for the fifteen-minute check-in. If you leave without a number for the first, they will not start.
