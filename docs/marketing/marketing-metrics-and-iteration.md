# Marketing Metrics & Iteration

> **20 minutes, every Monday.** This is where marketing decisions get made — and the only place. Nothing changes mid-week.
>
> ⚠️ **You have zero clients and zero sends. Every conversion rate in any plan — including mine — is a guess until you have your own data.** The targets below are starting points to be replaced by observation, not benchmarks to be hit.

---

## 1 · The seven numbers

Seven, because you will actually look at seven. Twenty becomes a dashboard nobody opens.

| # | Metric | Where from | Week 4 target | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Warm messages sent** | Your count | 100 | ⭐ The only input **entirely** in your control. If this is low, nothing else matters |
| **2** | **Reply rate** | Replies ÷ sent | >15% | Below 10% = the message is wrong, not the list |
| **3** | **Subscribers** | beehiiv | 50 | Direction, not size. 50 real > 500 rented |
| **4** | **Open rate** | beehiiv | >40% | Below 30% = subject lines or a cold list |
| **5** | **Replies to the newsletter** | Your inbox | 3 | ⭐ Worth more than 100 opens |
| **6** | **CONVERSATIONS** | Your count | **10** | ⭐⭐ **The one that predicts revenue** |
| **7** | **CLIENTS** | The product | **1** | Everything above is a leading indicator of this |

### Deliberately not tracked

| ❌ | Why not |
|---|---|
| Followers | Rented. Cannot be exported, cannot be emailed |
| Impressions | Nobody ever bought anything because of an impression |
| Likes | Feels like progress. Isn't |
| Click-through rate alone | Only meaningful next to what happened after the click |
| "Engagement rate" | A platform's number, optimised for the platform |

**A conversation is:** a real back-and-forth with a named human about their pipeline. **Not** a like, not a reply that says "thanks", not a newsletter open.

## 2 · The Monday review — 20 minutes

Same order, every week. Write the answers down; unwritten reviews become vibes.

### Step 1 · The numbers (5 min)

| Metric | Last wk | This wk | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm messages | | | |
| Reply rate | | | |
| Subscribers | | | |
| Open rate | | | |
| Newsletter replies | | | |
| **Conversations** | | | |
| **Clients** | | | |

### Step 2 · The four questions (10 min)

1. **What produced a conversation this week?** Name the specific thing. If nothing did, say that plainly.
2. **What's the bottleneck?** Not enough messages? Messages not landing? Landing but not converting? **Name one.**
3. **What did I hear three times?** Three of anything — an objection, a question, a confusion — is a signal, not noise. It becomes next week's content **and** a copy or product change.
4. **What am I avoiding?** Usually the honest answer. Usually the important one.

### Step 3 · One change (5 min)

**ONE.** Write it down. Do it this week. Measure it next week.

> **This week's change:** ___________________
> **What I expect to happen:** ___________________
> **How I'll know:** ___________________

⚠️ **Changing three things at once means learning nothing.** You will never know which one worked — and at your volumes you cannot separate them statistically.

## 3 · More → Better → New

From your own playbook §19. **Use it in this exact order every week** — it is the antidote to chasing novelty.

```
Results stalled?
   │
   ├─ 1. MORE?    Am I doing enough of what already works?
   │              5 messages/day → 10. Same message.
   │              ✅ Cheapest fix. Try this FIRST, always.
   │
   ├─ 2. BETTER?  Is the thing itself good enough?
   │              Rewrite the message. Fix the landing page.
   │              ✅ Only after MORE has been genuinely tried.
   │
   └─ 3. NEW?     Only after MORE and BETTER are exhausted.
                  New channel, new audience, new format.
                  ⚠️ This is where most founders start. It's why they stall.
```

⚠️ **"I've tried MORE" usually means one week.** One week is not a test at your volumes. **Give it three.**

## 4 · Rules of thumb

### Kill it when

| Thing | Kill after |
|---|---|
| A post format | 5 attempts, zero conversations |
| A message/script | 30 sends, <5% reply rate |
| A content pillar | 4 weeks, no engagement, no conversations |
| An ad creative | 1,000 impressions, CTR <0.5% |
| A whole channel | 8 weeks of real effort, zero conversations |

### Double down when

| Signal | Do this |
|---|---|
| One post gets 3× your normal replies | Write 3 more on the same idea |
| One objection comes up 3× | It becomes a post, a newsletter issue **and** a copy change |
| One warm message style out-replies the rest | Make it the default. Stop writing the others |
| A subscriber replies unprompted | ⭐ **Reply within the hour.** This is a lead |

### Increase spend when *(ads only, gated)*

- Cost-per-subscriber has been stable for 5 straight days, **and**
- Those subscribers are actually replying, **and**
- You could lose next month's budget without it mattering
- **Scale 20–30%. Never double.**

## 5 · The monthly look-back

Once a month, 45 minutes, different questions to the weekly.

| ☐ | Question |
|---|---|
| ☐ | **Which single activity produced the most conversations?** Do more of it |
| ☐ | **Which produced none?** Kill it. Actually kill it — don't "pause" it |
| ☐ | Is the ICP still right? Who's actually replying vs who I aimed at |
| ☐ | Is the message still right? What words do *they* use? |
| ☐ | Re-read the one-page plan (`MARKETING-PLAN.md` §1). **Change it here or not at all** |
| ☐ | Prune the list — hard bounces, 90-day non-openers |
| ☐ | Marketing spend vs the cost floor (**$352/mo**) |
| ☐ | **One conversation with someone who didn't buy.** Ask why. ⭐ Best 20 minutes of the month |

## 6 · What each stall means

A diagnostic table, because "it's not working" is not actionable.

| Symptom | Most likely cause | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sending, no replies | Message is about **you**, not them | Rewrite around their problem. Cut every "we" |
| Replies, no conversations | The ask is too big | Make it smaller. *"Want me to run your first 10?"* not *"book a demo"* |
| Conversations, no clients | Wrong people, or the product doesn't match the promise | **Ask them directly.** They will tell you |
| Subscribers, no opens | Subject lines, or they forgot subscribing | Better subjects. Send **more** often, not less |
| Opens, no replies | You're not asking | End every issue with one real question |
| Everything flat | **Not enough volume to see a signal** | MORE, for three weeks, before changing anything |

## 7 · Honest expectations

Because a plan that promises too much gets abandoned in week 3.

| Timeframe | Realistic |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Nothing. You are building |
| Week 2 | 2–5 replies. Maybe one conversation |
| Week 4 | 5–10 conversations. **Possibly** one client |
| Month 3 | The message is working or it clearly isn't. You will know |
| Month 6 | If consistent: predictable inbound. If not: still guessing |

⚠️ **The single most common failure is quitting in week 5**, when the work has been done but the compounding hasn't started. **The newsletter is worth almost nothing at issue 4 and a great deal at issue 40.**

⚠️ **And one specific to you:** getting a client *too fast*, before A11's money journeys are walked, is a worse outcome than getting one slowly. **Warm-first is deliberate.**
