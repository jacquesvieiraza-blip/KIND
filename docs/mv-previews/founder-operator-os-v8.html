02 · Role architecture

Use expensive judgement only where judgement is actually needed.

Founder owns the irreducible work

DirectionWhat K.I.N.D. is, priorities, pricing, positioning and where the company goes.

Customer truthUnderstand what clients actually experience, value and struggle with.

Technical support model

Fable · heavy judgementJunior · bounded executionOpus · acceleratorSecond eyes · review

Fable handles architecture, difficult debugging, compliance-sensitive paths, production/db discrepancies and expensive reasoning.

Junior developer handles small features, tests, UI, bug fixes, scripts and bounded PR work.

Opus accelerates approved work but does not redefine the task.

Second eyes reviews independently and reports findings without becoming the builder.

09 · Founder absence mode

The company must remain founder-led without becoming founder-dependent.

Continuity principle

When the founder is unavailable, the objective changes from improvement to continuity.

Keep customers served. Keep money moving. Keep production stable. Record exceptions. Defer non-essential change until the founder returns.

No speculative builds No architecture changes No pricing changes No non-essential migrations No new product promises

Absence-safe company

Founder-led → strategic authority remains with you.

System-operated → routine work continues through defined systems.

Human-supported → deputies and contractors own bounded operating processes.

Exception-escalated → only material problems rise upward.

Absence-safe → short-term continuity does not depend on your immediate availability.

Backup roles during founder absence

Deputy OperatorRuns inbox, CRM, onboarding, routine customer operations, contractor coordination and the daily control check.
Customer Success BackupKeeps active customers supported, follows approved playbooks and escalates genuine problems.
Sales ContractorsContinue approved offers, pricing and follow-up. No new discounts, promises or bespoke commitments.
Technical GuardianEmergency incidents only: diagnose, restore, rollback or stabilise. No roadmap development during absence mode.
Accountant / BookkeeperKeeps billing, routine financial admin, records and supplier obligations current.
AI + AutomationContinue approved repetitive workflows, monitoring and reporting. No autonomous expansion of scope.

Authority matrix

Deputy may Reply to customers, reschedule onboarding, chase invoices, allocate routine work, use approved playbooks, and pause risky activity when something looks wrong.
Deputy may not Change pricing, make new product promises, approve legal changes, deploy code, approve migrations, or make material commercial exceptions.
Technical Guardian may Investigate incidents, restore service, rollback to a known-good release and use emergency access under the documented runbook.
Technical Guardian may not Introduce features, refactor architecture, change product behaviour, or use an outage as permission to redesign the system.
Sales may Sell the approved offer, use approved commercial terms and continue existing prospect follow-up.
Sales may not Create new discounts, unusual contract promises, implementation commitments or unsupported product claims.

Three operating modes

Mode A · 1–2 days

Founder temporarily offline

Business runs normally. Routine decisions continue. Non-urgent exceptions queue for your return.

Mode B · 1–2 weeks

Founder away

Development slows or freezes. Customers, sales and billing continue. Deputy runs the control check and only serious exceptions escalate.

Mode C · unexpected

Founder unavailable

Continuity runbook activates. Customer care, money, outreach controls and emergency technical support continue under strict boundaries.

The continuity runbook must answer immediately

CustomersWho is active, what has been promised, what is due, and who needs attention?
SalesWhat offers, pricing, promises and commitments are currently approved?
MoneyWhere billing lives, what must be paid, and who owns routine financial administration?
OutreachHow is outreach paused safely if risk appears?
Technical emergencyWho can diagnose, rollback and restore service, and where is the runbook?
Credentials + recoveryWhere secure emergency access and account recovery procedures are stored.

Critical credentials should remain in a secure password or secrets manager with appropriate emergency-access arrangements, not in ordinary docs, chats or hand-written notes.

27 · End-state design principle

The goal is leverage, not a headcount stunt.
Build a company that can become extremely valuable while preserving founder control, high leverage, strong margins, speed and simplicity.

Do not optimise for “one employee” at all costs. Optimise for the smallest permanent organisation that can safely support the value, risk and customer base of the business.

46 · Constraint OS

Do not improve everything at once. Find the constraint, remove it, then find the next one.

The operating question

WHAT IS THE CURRENT CONSTRAINT?

At any point, one bottleneck is likely limiting the company more than everything else. The founder's job is to identify it, concentrate resources, remove it, measure the effect, then repeat.

Constraint loop

1
Find itWhat is actually limiting growth or delivery?
2
FocusConcentrate attention, capital and people.
3
Remove itSolve the bottleneck, not the symptoms.
4
MeasureDid the constraint really move?
5
RepeatThe next constraint becomes visible.

Typical constraint sequence

No customersConstraint = acquisition. Solve demand and outreach.
Leads do not buyConstraint = offer or sales conversion.
Onboarding breaksConstraint = fulfilment and customer operations.
Customers churnConstraint = value, product fit or retention.
Founder is overwhelmedConstraint = delegation, systems and decision routing.
Growth stallsConstraint = distribution, market size, economics or product expansion.
Constraint rule
The five executive views tell you where to look. Constraint OS tells you what to attack first.

48 · Stage-Gate Operating Model

Move stages only when evidence proves the current stage has done its job.

Core rule

You do not move stages because time passed or because you feel busy. You move when objective evidence proves the current stage has done its job.

The stage tells you where dominant founder attention belongs. The gate tells you when it is safe to shift that attention.

49 · The seven stage gates

Each stage has an entry condition, a proof marker and an exit trigger.
STAGE 1 · YOU DO IT Enter: real customers begin using K.I.N.D. · Marker: you can explain who buys, why, what they expect, where they struggle and what creates value · Exit: repeated customer journeys reveal stable patterns rather than guesses
↓
STAGE 2 · MAKE IT REPEATABLE Enter: patterns are visible · Marker: one ICP, one main offer, one sales path, one onboarding path and one fulfilment path handle most normal cases · Exit: another competent person can follow the process without you inventing it again
↓
STAGE 3 · TURN KNOWLEDGE INTO SYSTEM Enter: repeatable work still depends on founder memory or coordination · Marker: CRM, GitHub, customer systems, playbooks, automations and dashboards carry the process · Exit: the system knows what should happen next without founder routing
↓
STAGE 4 · REMOVE YOURSELF FROM NORMAL CASES Enter: the systems exist but founder execution is still too high · Marker: VA, CS, sales, junior dev and AI handle normal cases · Exit: the company operates normally for at least one working week without founder routine intervention
↓
STAGE 5 · BUILD DISTRIBUTION Enter: delivery works and customers receive repeatable value · Marker: more than one acquisition source creates qualified opportunities · Exit: growth is no longer primarily limited by founder-led prospecting and selling
↓
STAGE 6 · EXPAND CUSTOMER ECONOMICS Enter: acquisition and basic retention are functioning · Marker: customers renew, expand, use more, move tiers or refer · Exit: existing customers become a meaningful source of growth
↓
STAGE 7 · FOUNDER BECOMES CAPITAL ALLOCATOR Enter: people and systems can operate and grow the company without routine founder execution · Marker: most founder time goes to direction, allocation, major customers, product, partnerships and strategic decisions · Exit: none — defend this operating position

50 · Gate evidence

Do not confuse activity with proof.
Stage 1 gate · Customer truth Know who buys, why they buy, what they expect, time to first value, where they struggle, why they stay and why they leave. Use roughly 5–10 real customer journeys as an early evidence base, not as a magic statistical threshold.
Stage 2 gate · Operational repeatability Another competent person can follow the process. Target roughly 80% of normal cases flowing through the defined path, with the remaining exceptions known and explicit.
Stage 3 gate · System ownership Leads, onboarding, support, code changes, billing events and other normal work move because systems trigger them, not because the founder remembers them.
Stage 4 gate · Founder removal Run a controlled five-working-day founder-absence test. Customers, leads, onboarding, invoices and incidents continue under defined authority without uncontrolled decisions.
Stage 5 gate · Distribution independence At least one non-founder acquisition source reliably produces qualified opportunities, then build toward a second. Track pipeline, customers, CAC and conversion by source.
Stage 6 gate · Economic compounding Retention, revenue per customer, expansion, referrals and margins improve. Existing customers contribute growth rather than every month starting from zero.
Stage 7 gate · Founder leverage Founder calendar is dominated by high-leverage work rather than routine operations. A useful target is around 80% high-leverage attention and 20% or less operational exceptions.

Every idea must earn attention against the current gate.

Do now

Park

53 · Gate stability rule

Never advance because of one lucky customer, one huge sale or one unusually quiet week.

A stage gate is passed only when the exit marker remains true across several customer journeys, a meaningful cohort, or multiple weeks — whichever fits the metric.

Stage changes should reflect a durable operating condition, not a temporary spike.

The progression loop
The company advances through evidence, not activity.
STAGE → CONSTRAINT → METRIC → WORK → EVIDENCE → GATE → NEXT STAGE

54 · Revenue → Capacity → People OS

Revenue gives permission to hire. Capacity tells you when to hire.

The missing people layer

Do not add humans because an org chart says a business of your size “should” have them. Add capacity when a measurable system reaches its safe limit.

AI, VA, commission sales, customer success, junior development and specialists carry execution. The question is not whether humans appear; it is when each function has earned dedicated human capacity.

Hiring philosophy
Revenue funds capacity. Constraint reveals the bottleneck. Hiring or automation increases capacity. Then growth resumes.
REVENUE → CAPACITY → CONSTRAINT → HIRE / AUTOMATE → GROW

57 · Commission-sales scaling advantage

Sales capacity can scale earlier than fixed payroll — but delivery must absorb what sales creates.

Why sales can scale differently

Under the current planning model, commission salespeople receive 25% of eligible lead revenue. If a salesperson sells nothing, direct commission cost is essentially zero. If they sell $1M of eligible lead revenue, $250k goes to commission and $750k remains before delivery and operating costs.

The likely bottleneck shift

Sales can scale ahead of some fixed headcount only if onboarding, delivery, support and product capacity can safely absorb the customers being sold. The dangerous bottleneck may become delivery capacity rather than sales payroll.

61 · Founder-bottleneck test

Headcount only works if it actually removes founder dependency.

Adding people without adding functional ownership simply recreates the founder bottleneck at a larger scale.

At Stage 7, the target remains that most founder attention is high-leverage work rather than coordinating individual workers. A practical marker is roughly 80% high-leverage founder attention and 20% or less operational exceptions.

FINAL PEOPLE PRINCIPLE
Build the smallest permanent organisation that can safely support the value, risk and customer base of K.I.N.D. — then let measurable capacity constraints decide what gets added next.

64 · Observe → Act → Request → Approve

Separate visibility from authority.
1
Observe Operator sees only the information needed for the task.
2
Act Operator performs actions explicitly allowed by role.
3
Request Higher-risk changes become requests, not direct controls.
4
Approve Founder or authorised senior role approves material actions.

Examples

SuppressionCS may request or perform suppression for a specific contact; no need for access to the global suppression system.
Plan adjustmentCS submits a request rather than editing account limits or pricing directly.
RefundOperator may act within an approved threshold; above it becomes an approval request.
Technical issueCS records evidence and escalates; they do not enter production systems to debug it.

65 · Role & Permission Architecture

Permissions belong to roles, not individual memory.
FounderFull company control and strategic visibility.
CS AgentAssigned clients + approved customer-success actions.
CS Lead / DeputyBroader client operations, team visibility and bounded escalation authority.
SalesAssigned prospects/accounts, approved pricing, deal flow and sales tools.

Technical GuardianApproved technical systems and emergency controls required for resilience.

Least-privilege rule

Access should be granted by role + assignment + action scope.

Example: a CS agent may read customer-success data for clients A, C and F, but cannot read every client simply because they are logged into Vida.

66 · Security must exist behind the UI

Hiding a menu item is not an access-control system.

Unsafe pattern

“If role = CS, hide Revenue.”

A hidden screen does not prove the underlying API or database cannot still be called.

Required pattern

Authorise at the data and API layer.

Use authentication, role claims, assignment records and database/API policies so unauthorised data cannot be returned even if someone bypasses the UI.

Conceptual assignment model

operator_client_assignments

operator_id → client_id → role → allowed scope

The permission system should answer both which records a person can access and which actions they can perform.
