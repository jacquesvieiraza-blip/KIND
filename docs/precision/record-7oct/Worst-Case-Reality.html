The worst case: 1 meeting per 1,300 people

One client, 10 meetings in one month, run the fastest way we found (bought warmed inboxes, 3 emails per person, our own sender). Your cost figures. Nothing is decided — this is for thinking. 7 Oct 2026.

In one paragraph

At 1 in 1,300 one client needs 13,000 people, 39,000 emails, about 123 inboxes and roughly 390 replies handled in a month, and costs $2,450–5,540 that month. Break-even is $258–583 a meeting. At your prices you lose at $99 and $199, and $299 only scrapes +$389 in the best case. One person cannot answer 390 replies a month. At the worst case, this business cannot operate at your prices. It works only if your emails do far better than the agency average — around 1 in 250 or better — and nobody has measured that yet.

What the worst case means in practice

  • The work, not just the money: 123 inboxes on ~40 domains for one client, and ~390 replies in a month — a full-time job for one client.
  • Your sending limit: 39,000 emails is ~1,800–2,400 a day for one client; your all-client brake is 1,000.
  • The only prices that survive: roughly $300–600 a meeting — about what agencies charge (see the market research).
  • Same-month promise: even with everything bought, planning for 10 gives only an even chance; being ~85% sure needs ~17,000 people and ~160 inboxes.

What would change the picture

  1. A better hit rate — only emailing people with a buying signal; a sharper offer. At 1 in 250 the $199 and $299 bands work (see the table). Unproven for UK clients.
  2. A higher price — about $260–580 a meeting covers the worst case; UK agencies charge £150–500 plus a retainer.
  3. No same-month promise — e.g. 10 meetings over 60–90 days with fewer inboxes.
  4. Measure first — the ~$260, 5-week House test tells you which world you are in before you spend more.

Market research: is 1 in 1,300 right?

Verdict: 1 in 1,300 is not absurd — it is a realistic floor. A large agency's whole client book (Belkins, 2025: 7.5M emails, ~1,200 meetings) works out at about 1 in 1,250–1,570 people, and an independent 147,000-email campaign landed at about 1 in 1,225. The largest independent per-person dataset (1.37M contacts, 0.64% positive replies) gives about 1 in 300–500 when replies are handled well. A tight campaign — only people with a buying signal, small-company founders, 3+ follow-ups, a soft "interested?" ask, fast replies — realistically reaches 1 in 150–400; 1 in 100 is top-10%. Only 60–80% of booked meetings actually happen. Plan on 1 in 500; stress-test at 1 in 1,300.
