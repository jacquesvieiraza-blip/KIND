3. Objective

Get-Kind should evolve from a primarily contact-enrichment pipeline into a multi-source evidence, signal and timing engine.

The target is not “50 data vendors”.

The target is to combine multiple evidence classes so the system can explain: who the right person is, why the company fits, why now is relevant, how confident the data is, what the lead cost, and whether the prospect is eligible for outreach.

8. Conditional enrichment waterfall

The target is a confidence- and cost-aware resolver rather than a fixed provider chain.

1. Search local lead_pool
2. Evaluate freshness and confidence
3. If sufficient → stop
4. If insufficient → query preferred approved provider for this geography / ICP / field
5. Compare returned assertion with existing evidence
6. If confidence threshold met → stop
7. Otherwise optionally query a secondary approved provider
8. Verify email only when the prospect is otherwise worth pursuing
9. Record total acquisition cost and every source used

Verify whether provider selection is currently configurable by geography, ICP, field, historical performance or data quality.

18. Meeting Yield Engine — the commercial objective

Primary commercial outcome: qualified held meetings that create pipeline.

Do not optimise the product for raw email volume, raw reply rate or even raw booked meetings alone. The retained-value metric should be the number of qualified meetings that actually happen, with downstream opportunity and revenue attribution where the client supplies it.

The system should therefore distinguish:

sent
→ delivered
→ opened / engaged where lawfully measured
→ replied
→ positive reply
→ meeting offered
→ meeting booked
→ meeting confirmed
→ meeting held
→ opportunity created
→ reseller / client conversion
→ won revenue

Claude must verify which stages are represented in code and data today, and whether Get-Kind can optimise campaigns against the stages that matter commercially.

19. Campaign objective hierarchy

The campaign engine should optimise in this order:

  1. Qualified held meetings — primary optimisation target.
  2. Booked meetings — leading indicator.
  3. Positive replies — leading indicator of message-market fit.
  4. Reply rate — diagnostic only; negative replies should not improve a campaign score.
  5. Delivery rate — necessary constraint, not the business outcome.
  6. Client opportunity / reseller conversion — downstream quality signal when available.
  7. Won revenue — ultimate source/campaign quality signal when supplied by the client CRM.
Do not optimise blindly for meeting count.

A campaign that books many low-quality meetings can increase cancellations, no-shows and client churn. Meeting quality, show rate and downstream conversion must influence campaign learning.

24. Meeting booking friction

Once a prospect is interested, the system should minimise the distance between positive intent and a confirmed meeting.

Verify whether Get-Kind supports:

  • client-specific booking links;
  • server-validated availability;
  • timezone-aware slots;
  • short default meeting duration where appropriate;
  • immediate booking from a positive reply;
  • human-approved reply drafts that contain relevant availability;
  • clear confirmation and reschedule/cancel paths;
  • deduplication so one prospect cannot accidentally create multiple meetings;
  • meeting attribution back to campaign, sequence step, source and signal.

The existing signed booking token and slot validation should be verified as part of this flow.

25. Positive-reply to meeting conversion

A major commercial KPI should be:

positive_reply_to_booking_rate
=
booked_meetings / qualified_positive_replies

If this number is weak, the issue is usually no longer lead sourcing. Claude should verify whether Get-Kind can measure where interested prospects are lost:

  • positive reply received but not answered quickly;
  • human approval waiting too long;
  • booking link not sent;
  • calendar has too little availability;
  • timezone mismatch;
  • prospect asks a question and receives no useful response;
  • booking flow fails;
  • meeting gets booked but later cancelled.

Report whether latency from positive reply → human approval → outbound reply → booked meeting is recorded.

26. No-show reduction

Booked meetings are not enough. Verify whether the platform can improve held meeting rate.

Desired controls include:

  • confirmation immediately after booking;
  • calendar invitation status tracking;
  • optional compliant reminders;
  • easy rescheduling;
  • meeting-status ingestion from the client calendar or CRM;
  • no-show and cancellation reasons;
  • campaign/source attribution for no-show rate.

The system should measure:

show_rate = held_meetings / booked_meetings
