Get-Kind — Vida Post-10-Client Autonomous Operations Verification

Purpose: read-only Claude Code verification of whether Vida can safely evolve into a more autonomous operations layer after Get-Kind has approximately 10 live clients and the early operational edge cases have been observed, logged and understood.

This is a post-10-client architecture/readiness review. It is not an instruction to automate Vida now.

1. Hard instruction to Claude Code

READ-ONLY AUTONOMY READINESS REVIEW. DO NOT CHANGE THE CODE.

Do not edit, create, delete, rename, reformat or move repository files. Do not generate or apply patches. Do not run migrations. Do not modify configuration, environment values, infrastructure or databases. Do not commit, push or open a pull request.

Your job is to verify whether the current architecture could support a safer autonomous Vida after the first ~10 clients have exposed real operational edge cases.

2. Why this is deliberately post-10-client

The first clients are intended to expose the real failure modes that are difficult to predict from code or planning alone.

Do not automate unknown behaviour.

Before Vida becomes materially autonomous, Get-Kind should first observe real client operations, catalogue the “bad eggs”, and distinguish normal repeatable work from exceptional work requiring human judgement.

Examples of early operational edge cases to expect:

  • bad or contradictory ICP inputs;
  • clients changing direction mid-campaign;
  • weak or misleading campaign signals;
  • mailbox/deliverability anomalies;
  • positive replies requiring nuanced interpretation;
  • compliance uncertainty;
  • unexpected CRM or data-provider failures;
  • client-specific exceptions;
  • booking/calendar failures;
  • suppression or dedup edge cases;
  • campaigns that appear healthy on replies but do not book meetings;
  • clients whose approved strategy produces poor results;
  • unusual spend or enrichment behaviour.

9. Hard human-approval boundaries

Vida should stop and escalate for material changes such as:

  • new country/jurisdiction;
  • new outreach channel;
  • new client proposition or materially new campaign claim;
  • budget increase beyond approved limits;
  • compliance uncertainty;
  • change to permanent suppression policy;
  • security incident;
  • client complaint;
  • high-risk or legally sensitive reply;
  • system behaviour outside learned/approved patterns;
  • mass campaign pause/restart above a defined threshold;
  • material change to client Meeting Brief.

Claude must verify whether these types of approval boundaries can be represented and enforced.

10. Reversibility requirement

Autonomous actions should be reversible wherever practical.

Claude should verify whether the following can be rolled back safely:

  • campaign allocation change;
  • campaign pause/resume;
  • sequence activation;
  • lead assignment;
  • draft generation;
  • non-destructive configuration changes.

Irreversible actions such as sending an email, charging money, deleting data or changing suppression state require stronger controls and explicit audit evidence.
